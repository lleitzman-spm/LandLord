# Brief: incidents — weather_magic

**Your files:** `src/data/incidents/weather.ts` and `src/data/incidents/magic.ts` (keep the exported const names: `WEATHER_INCIDENTS`, `MAGIC_INCIDENTS`).
**Read:** `src/data/schema.ts` (IncidentDef, OutcomeDef, Requires, Effect: read every comment), `ids.ts` (ICONS, VISUALS, building types, species, trades, traits, tags, pets, NPCS: you may only use ids that exist), `src/sim/types.ts` (DecisionType, DECISION_OPTIONS, RankId), `src/data/incidents/system.ts` and `examples.ts` (**the quality bar and the exact shape: study them**), `buildings.ts`, `ranks.ts` (what exists at each rank), `docs/STYLE.md` (the voice, the costume table, species as tenants), `docs/BIBLE.md`.

**How to write one.** Start from a real thing that happens to people who manage rental property, find its medieval costume, and play it dead straight. Give the petitioner a reasonable point. Make every outcome defensible, with honest trade-offs in the effects (coin against happiness against renown against condition against time). Effects must be modest and proportionate to the rank: at Reeve a crown matters (rents are 8 to 16); at Baron tens of crowns. Typical magnitudes: happiness ±3 to ±15, renown ±1 to ±3, favor ±2 to ±6, condition ±4 to ±20. `intent` is a plain sentence for a model deciding which outcome the ruler meant. `say` is what a ruler might actually say aloud. `reply` and `result` carry the comedy. Roughly a third should be audiences (`audience: true`, with 2 or 3 `opening` lines and a `stance`); the rest are map pins with exactly one `default: true` outcome and usually a `job`. Use `requires` so an incident only appears where it makes sense, `seasons` where apt, `minRank` to spread them (about 45% reeve, 35% knight, 20% baron), `cooldownMonths` of 8 to 20, and `weight` (0.3 rare and special, 1 ordinary). Give visuals to the ones that deserve to be seen. Chains are welcome: a `spawn` effect to another incident **in your own file** (set that follow-up incident to `weight: 0` so only the chain spawns it). Vary structure and length; do not let them fall into one rhythm.

**Check before you finish:** `node tools/check.mjs src/data/incidents` and `npx tsx tools/validate-content.ts` (it lints ids, placeholders, lengths, dashes, option tags; errors about incidents in other files are not yours, but every error naming one of your ids must be gone). Then reread all of yours once, cold, as a player: cut or rewrite any that are merely fine. In your report give the count and your three favourites by title.

**Your territory: at least 30 incidents.** `weather.ts` (about fourteen, with `seasons`): hard frost and burst pipes ("the well has frozen and so has the man who went to check"), a thatch-lifting gale, hail, the spring flood, a heatwave and the request for "a breeze", snowed-in tenants, autumn leaves and whose job they are, mud season, a lightning-struck oak, drought and the well rota, fog in which a tenant has lost their own house, the first frost and the oath you made about that roof. `magic.ts` (about sixteen, mostly Knight and Baron): **you must define `wizards_parting_gift`** (weight 0; target `building`; what the wizard left behind after the surety was kept), which `examples.ts` chains to. Then: an alchemist's kitchen fire and the dragon adjuster, a tower that is larger inside and the rent implications, a cursed hovel nobody will lease, a haunting where the ghost has a lease older than yours, a tenant turned into a toad who still owes rent, a toadstool ring on the green, a familiar that files complaints, a love potion in the well, the apothecary's smoke changing the colour of the laundry, a prophecy about a rent rise, a scrying mirror used to watch for the bailiff, a small portal in a pantry and the question of who it belongs to.
