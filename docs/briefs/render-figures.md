# Brief: render-figures (the living figurines)

**Your files:** everything under `src/render/figures/`, `src/render/townlife.ts`, `src/render/actors.ts`, `src/dev/gallery-figures.ts`.
**Read:** `src/render/api.ts` (FigureSpec, Figure, Env, RenderCtx), the `FigureLayer` / `LayerHelpers` shapes in `townlife.ts` (keep them), `palette.ts`, `src/sim/types.ts` (Actor, Job, Tenant, StaffMember, SimEvent), `src/data/ids.ts` (ANIMS, EMOTES, TRADES, SPECIES, NPCS), `src/dev/fixture.ts`.

The owner asked for little people that feel **alive**. The answer agreed with them is "living figurines": carved game pieces whose life comes from behaviour. This is the piece of the game most likely to make someone smile. Make it so.

**The figure** (`createFigure`): a turned-wood peg body (lathe), a round head with a **painted face that moves** (eyes that blink and glance, brows and mouth that follow `setMood`, a talking mouth), **two floating mitten hands** (no arms), no legs. About 0.42 tall, chunky, readable from far away. Species: halflings small with big bare feet peeking out and curly hair; dwarves short, wide, bearded (all of them); goblins small, green, huge ears, sharp grin; elves tall, slim, pointed ears, serene; ogres big (about 0.7), grey-green, tiny hat, gentle eyes. A hat, apron or tool for each of the 28 trades; liveries for the four delegates (mason: leather cap and apron, hammer; bailiff: dark coat, tall hat, ring of keys; crier: feathered cap, bell; steward: robe, ledger and quill); the ruler by rank (plain cloak and circlet, then coronet, then crown and ermine) and slightly larger; distinct looks for all fifteen NPC ids; children. Seeds vary skin, hair, cloth, proportions.

**Animation**: all twenty `AnimId`s, procedural: a waddle (rock side to side, hands swinging), run, hammer, sweep, carry (a sack or purse held up), wave, shake_fist, cheer, bow, sleep (slumped, a slow breath), talk, cower, knock, read, count, shrug, dance, pray, point; `react` one-shots layered on top; head `lookAt` with the body following lazily; selection bounce and ring. Small emote sprites pop above the head for `emote` events (canvas-drawn: heart, anger scribble, coin, Zzz, music notes, question, alert, sweat, skull, idea).

**The actor layer** (`createActorLayer`): a figure for every `state.actors` entry, smoothly following the sim position, heading from movement, animation from actor state and job kind (build/repair → hammer; collect → carry a purse; show → point; evict → knock then shake_fist; waiting petitioner → shifts weight, looks at the ruler). The ruler is unmistakable.

**Town life** (`createTownLife`): cosmetic figures for housed households (the head, sometimes a second adult or a child by household size, capped overall), living a day across `env.monthPhase`: at dawn they come to the door (those with a purse out stand beside it and wave when the collector nears); through the day they go about the town with purpose (to the well, market stall, tavern, garden, fields, their own shop if they lease one), stop in pairs to gossip, sit, sweep; at dusk they head home; at night they are indoors. They react: scatter and cower from a griffin or fire nearby, cheer at a feast, shake a fist at the bailiff if unhappy, look up when selected, slump when miserable, skip when delighted. Arrivals walk in from the gate with a sack; the departing walk out (the evicted slowly, looking back). Use your own small grid pathfinder over `state.map.tiles` (the sim's may still be a placeholder). Movement follows `env.speed` (when paused they idle in place). `positionOf`, `pick`, `setSelected` work for tenant ids.

**Performance**: 120 figures at 60 fps on an integrated GPU: share geometries and materials, keep per-frame work tiny, no per-frame allocation.

**Dev gallery.** `src/dev/gallery-figures.ts`: your own lit scene (no dependency on render/index): a line-up of every species and delegate and the ruler at each rank; a row cycling every animation; a little crowd walking and gossiping. Use `--shots` to capture motion. Look at the shots; iterate until they have charm and personality at game distance and in close-up.
