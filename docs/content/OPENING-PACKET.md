# Opening packet: The Bellwether purse

**Status:** proposal for the first 20 to 30 minutes. Names and figures are working values, not canon.

## Inheritance and authority

Your aunt, Dame Elinor Vale, died without children. Her will leaves you **360 crowns**, her property ledger and a brass key to Bellwether. The Crown's clerk confirms the inheritance. Bellwether remains under its lord, who keeps authority over the fief, tribute and assessors. You may buy property, make leases and order work under existing law. Local jurisdiction is a later appointment to earn, not a gift in the will.

Elinor's accounts are settled. No property or rent passes with the purse. Her ledger has deeds, leases and work entries for the three neighbourhoods, but grants no rights. The abbey holds a footpath easement in Abbey Fold; the guild sets its market rules in Market Lane.

## Choose where to begin

Choose one neighbourhood, then reveal its three offers. These named opportunities occur only in the selected neighbourhood; the other two have distinct future leads.

**Quay Row** lies by the tidal landing. Boat hands and fish sellers seek nearby homes. Rents can be strong; salt damp wears timber. The reeve coordinates drain work.

**Market Lane** runs behind the guild market. Tradespeople seek homes and workshops. Demand is steady; guild measures and fees apply.

**Abbey Fold** borders the abbey orchard. It is quiet, with gardeners and carters. A deeded footpath crosses one boundary. The abbey can explain its terms for a fee or a records visit.

Each selected neighbourhood has three opportunities in the price and rent bands below. Figures are provisional. Offers may be declined; no loan or staged payment is assumed.

Any one or two purchases leave ample liquidity. All three cost 285 crowns; their 26 crowns of listed repairs leave **49 crowns**. One, two or all three can be bought without future rent or credit. Rent arrives only on Rent Day, according to each lease's start and terms.

## Investigation, leasing and work

Make deeds, leases, accounts and work entries searchable, showing author and date. A mason's entry reports what he observed then; a tenant's statement records her report. Inspection adds current observations and links to past work without changing it. Searching takes minutes; personal inspection takes hours; a professional costs money but can save time or notice more.

For example, inspect the Lantern Rooms personally for free until afternoon, or pay surveyor Elsbeth Rill 6 crowns for an assessment the same day. Personal inspection sees the cracked flue lining and damp. The old entry says the flue was patched two years ago, but does not establish the state of its hidden inner lining. Rill's survey identifies that lining as loose and cites the dated entry. Neither approach triggers automatic failure; the player chooses whether and when to repair.

The Lantern keeper runs the alehouse and pays under the existing lease. You do not buy stock or direct the business. Mara asks for the cottage leak repaired and offers a two-period renewal at the current rent. Agreeing records repair and term; declining leaves her lease in force and repair undecided. At the Sedge Yard, choose an applicant and agree rent, term, surety and work promises in writing.

Separate inspection cost from work price. Oswin Brace is skilled, careful and not always available. He charges 6 crowns to inspect and report; his scoped Lantern flue lining estimate is 12 crowns, with a two-day appointment. Bram Toller offers the same scope for 10 crowns at a later date. Inspection is not a 6-crown same-day repair against a 12-crown delayed repair. The order records scope, spending cap, date and workman. Completion records work done and remaining work. No work happens without authority.

## First two Rent Days

There is no forced countdown. Let the player walk the chosen streets, read the ledger, speak with tenants, inspect and compare offers. Time advances when chosen. At the first Rent Day, collect only rent due under leases that have started. Show each payment, any arrears and deductions plainly. A missed payment invites a conversation about the tenant's reason; it does not create an unexplained fine.

Between Rent Days, introduce one local relationship. At Abbey Fold, the abbey archivekeeper, Sister Ansel, can show the old footpath record. Separately, cellarer Brother Hugh asks for carts to use the path during a spring procession. Agree to a marked one-day route for an introduction to the abbey surveyor; negotiate an abbey-funded gate, spending time but retaining control over access; or decline the event while honouring the deed. This affects access and goodwill, without implying wrongdoing. At Market Lane, the guild asks to place a measure-stone on vacant frontage for a fee and public access. At Quay Row, the landing reeve offers shared drain clearance for a modest contribution and less damp nearby.

At the second Rent Day, show rent due, condition, work, promises and dated evidence. Offer next investment leads with prices, findings and likely effects. The player chooses what to pursue or set aside. No forced outcome or alert flood.
